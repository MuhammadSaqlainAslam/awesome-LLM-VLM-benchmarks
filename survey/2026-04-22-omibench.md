# OMIBench: Benchmarking Olympiad-Level Multi-Image Reasoning (2026)

## Problem
Existing multimodal benchmarks focus predominantly on single-image reasoning, leaving a critical gap in evaluating how well vision-language models handle problems requiring evidence distributed across multiple images. Olympiad-level science competitions naturally produce such multi-image problems, yet no benchmark had rigorously assessed whether LVLMs can reason over distributed visual evidence at expert difficulty.

## Method
**OMIBench** (arXiv: 2604.20806, April 22, 2026) is an Olympiad-level multi-image reasoning benchmark drawing from biology, chemistry, mathematics, and physics competition problems. Each problem requires integrating information from multiple images alongside textual prompts, with manually annotated rationales and standardised evaluation protocols.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- Problems sourced from international Olympiad competitions across four scientific disciplines
- Multi-image format: each problem distributes essential evidence across two or more images
- Manually annotated step-by-step rationales for each problem
- Evaluation protocol includes both accuracy and rationale quality scoring
- Multiple large vision-language models evaluated, including Gemini-3-Pro as the top performer

## Key Results

| Model | OMIBench Accuracy |
|---|---|
| Gemini-3-Pro | ~50% |
| Other frontier LVLMs | <50% |
| Human experts | >90% (estimated) |

- **Even the best model (Gemini-3-Pro) achieves only ~50%, indicating a substantial gap between current LVLMs and human Olympiad competitors**
- Multi-image distribution of evidence is a fundamental bottleneck that single-image benchmarks cannot reveal
- Performance gaps are consistent across all four scientific disciplines

## FoxBrain Relevance
Quality control and engineering inspection at Foxconn regularly involve cross-referencing multiple images — circuit board photographs, schematic diagrams, and defect maps — requiring the same kind of distributed visual reasoning OMIBench tests. A FoxBrain model scoring well on OMIBench would be better equipped to diagnose assembly failures by synthesising evidence from multiple inspection frames. The benchmark's Olympiad-level scientific problems also reflect the type of multi-step, high-precision reasoning needed for Foxconn's hardware verification workflows. Tracking OMIBench performance directly informs whether FoxBrain can handle complex visual cross-referencing in manufacturing settings.
