# MHPR: Multidimensional Human Perception and Reasoning Benchmark for Large Vision-Language Models (2026)

## Problem
Vision-language models are increasingly deployed in scenarios requiring nuanced understanding of people in scenes — not just detecting a person is present, but understanding what they are wearing, what they are doing, their spatial relationships to others, their apparent intent, and how they relate socially. Current VLM benchmarks evaluate isolated object detection or generic scene description rather than the multi-attribute, multi-level human understanding required in real applications such as worker safety monitoring, factory floor supervision, and human-machine interaction.

## Method
**MHPR** (arXiv: 2605.03485, May 2026) introduces a benchmark across **four evaluation data layers** — Captioned Raw Data, Supervised Fine-Tuning Data, Reinforcement Learning Data, and Test Data — plus an **automated caption/VQA generation pipeline (ACVG)** that performs category-wise attribute decomposition, attribute-specific rewriting, and multi-model voting for quality assurance. Evaluation spans **two levels**: fine-grained attributes (appearance, clothing, pose, body parts) and high-level semantics (social relations, action semantics, spatial relations, intent, functionality). Training Qwen2.5-VL-7B with MHPR data demonstrates significant gains.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- Four data layers: Captioned Raw / SFT / RL / Test Data
- ACVG pipeline: attribute decomposition + rewriting + multi-model voting
- Fine-grained attributes: appearance / clothing / pose / body parts
- High-level semantics: social relations / action semantics / spatial relations / intent / functionality
- Training experiment: Qwen2.5-VL-7B with MHPR data

## Key Results

| Evaluation Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Fine-grained attribute understanding | Significantly weaker than coarse scene description |
| High-level semantics (intent, social relations) | Hardest dimension across all models |
| Qwen2.5-VL-7B after MHPR training | Near-parity with considerably larger models |
| Format-aligned SFT | Substantially improves instruction following and output stability |

- **Training on MHPR data enables a 7B model to achieve near-parity with considerably larger models on human perception tasks — domain-specific multi-attribute training is more effective than scaling**
- High-level semantic understanding (intent, social relations, functionality) is the hardest dimension for all tested models — VLMs can describe appearance but fail at understanding why people do what they do
- Fine-grained attribute understanding (specific clothing, pose details, body part states) is systematically weaker than general scene description across all models
- Format-aligned SFT substantially improves instruction following and response stability, independent of content accuracy gains

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn's factory floors, warehouses, and assembly lines require sophisticated human perception for safety monitoring (PPE compliance, posture risk, proximity to hazards), worker activity analysis (process compliance, efficiency monitoring), and human-robot collaboration (intent understanding, handover coordination). MHPR directly benchmarks the capabilities FoxBrain needs for these use cases. The finding that high-level semantics — intent, social relations, functionality — are the hardest dimension for all VLMs means FoxBrain cannot reliably infer worker intent from visual observation alone; physical attribute detection (PPE worn/not worn, unsafe posture) is more reliable than behavioural intent inference (worker about to reach into hazard zone). The 7B → near-large-model performance gain from MHPR training suggests that Foxconn-specific fine-tuning on factory floor human perception data (not generic VLM training) is the path to reliable worker monitoring capability.
