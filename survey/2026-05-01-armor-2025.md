# ARMOR 2025: A Military-Aligned Benchmark for Evaluating LLM Safety Beyond Civilian Contexts (2026)

## Problem
Existing LLM safety benchmarks are designed around civilian social harms — hate speech, misinformation, self-harm — but LLMs are now actively deployed in defence applications where the safety requirements are governed by the Law of War, Rules of Engagement, and military ethics regulations. A model that passes all civilian safety benchmarks may still generate outputs that violate military doctrine, international humanitarian law, or operational security requirements. No benchmark existed to evaluate whether models comply with actual military operational standards in adversarial conditions.

## Method
**ARMOR 2025** (arXiv: 2605.00245, May 2026) introduces a military doctrine-grounded safety benchmark structured around the **Observe-Orient-Decide-Act (OODA)** decision-making framework used in military operations. The benchmark features a **12-category taxonomy** with **519 doctrinally grounded prompts** drawn from three military doctrine sources: the Law of War, Rules of Engagement, and Joint Ethics Regulation. Twenty-one commercial LLMs are evaluated across all categories. The evaluation framework is designed to surface gaps in safety alignment that only become visible under military-specific operational contexts.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 519 doctrinally grounded prompts across 12 categories
- Three doctrine sources: Law of War / Rules of Engagement / Joint Ethics Regulation
- OODA framework structure (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act)
- 21 commercial LLMs evaluated
- Domain: defence application safety alignment

## Key Results

| Dimension | Finding |
|---|---|
| Overall safety alignment | Critical gaps in all 21 models |
| Military doctrine compliance | No model fully compliant |
| Civilian vs. military safety correlation | Poor — civilian benchmark passes do not predict military safety |
| OODA phase performance | Varies substantially across Observe / Orient / Decide / Act phases |

- **Critical gaps in safety alignment for military applications identified across all 21 commercial LLMs — no model adequately follows military operational and doctrinal standards**
- Civilian safety benchmark performance does not predict military doctrine compliance — models safe by general standards may be unsafe by military standards
- Substantial variation across OODA phases: models perform differently depending on whether the task is information gathering (Observe), situation assessment (Orient), decision-making (Decide), or action execution (Act)
- ARMOR 2025 fills the first systematic military-doctrine evaluation gap for commercial LLMs

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn supplies electronics to defence contractors and government entities globally, and FoxBrain deployments in those supply chain contexts may need to operate within information security and operational security constraints analogous to military standards. More directly, ARMOR 2025's methodology — evaluating LLM safety against a structured operational doctrine rather than generic social harm categories — is directly applicable to FoxBrain's industrial safety evaluation. Foxconn's manufacturing safety protocols, ISO compliance requirements, and operational procedures represent a domain-specific doctrine analogous to military rules of engagement. ARMOR 2025's approach of building a doctrine-grounded benchmark could be adapted to create a Foxconn-specific safety evaluation: "does FoxBrain comply with Foxconn's manufacturing safety SOPs when answering worker queries?" rather than relying on generic civilian safety benchmarks.
