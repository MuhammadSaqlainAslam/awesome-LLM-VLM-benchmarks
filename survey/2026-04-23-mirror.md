# MIRROR: A Hierarchical Benchmark for Metacognitive Calibration in Large Language Models (2026)

## Problem
Autonomous AI systems are only as trustworthy as their self-awareness — a model that cannot accurately predict its own failures cannot reliably ask for help or defer to humans at the right moment. Yet no benchmark had systematically measured metacognitive calibration across multiple levels of self-knowledge and self-directed action, leaving a critical blind spot in the evaluation of agentic LLMs.

## Method
**MIRROR** (arXiv: 2604.19809, April 23, 2026) is a hierarchical benchmark for measuring metacognitive calibration in LLMs. It operationalises metacognition across four progressive levels — domain-specific self-knowledge, compositional self-prediction, action selection under uncertainty, and response to external calibration signals — through eight targeted experiments. Five independent behavioral measurement channels are used to ensure robustness, evaluated against 16 models from 8 laboratories across roughly 250,000 instances.

Authors: Jason Z Wang

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- Four hierarchical metacognitive levels, each with dedicated experimental protocols
- Eight experiments spanning domain knowledge, compositional tasks, and decision-making scenarios
- 16 models from 8 laboratories evaluated (expanded from original 15-model set)
- ~250,000 evaluation instances across five measurement channels
- Novel metrics: Compositional Calibration Error (CCE) and Confident Failure Rate (CFR)

## Key Results

| Metacognitive Level | Key Finding |
|---|---|
| Domain-specific self-knowledge | Above-chance but imperfect across all models |
| Compositional self-prediction | CCE 0.434–0.758 (all models fail) |
| Action selection under uncertainty | Models cannot translate self-knowledge into correct decisions |
| Response to calibration signals | Providing calibration scores yields no meaningful improvement |
| External metacognitive control | CFR reduced from 0.600 → 0.143 (76% improvement, temp=0) |

- **All 16 models universally fail at compositional self-prediction, with Compositional Calibration Error ranging 0.434–0.758 — models cannot predict their own performance when tasks span multiple domains**
- Models show above-chance domain-specific self-knowledge yet systematically fail to act on it — the self-knowledge to action translation is broken
- External metacognitive scaffolding (architectural constraints) reduces Confident Failure Rate by 76%; improved self-knowledge alone has no measurable effect
- The findings suggest fundamental LLM limitations in metacognition rather than mere knowledge deficits — self-calibration through prompting cannot fix this

## FoxBrain Relevance
Foxconn's FoxBrain agents are deployed in high-stakes manufacturing decisions — from defect classification to equipment maintenance scheduling — where overconfident errors are costly. MIRROR directly measures whether FoxBrain can accurately assess its own uncertainty before acting, which is essential for deciding when to escalate to a human operator versus proceeding autonomously. The benchmark's finding that external metacognitive scaffolding outperforms self-knowledge prompting guides the FoxBrain architecture team toward designing explicit confidence gating and human escalation triggers rather than relying on model self-reporting. Tracking FoxBrain's Compositional Calibration Error on MIRROR provides a quantitative safety signal for deployment readiness in multi-domain factory tasks.
