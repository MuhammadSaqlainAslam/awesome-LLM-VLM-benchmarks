# RadSaFE-200: Safety and Accuracy Follow Different Scaling Laws in Clinical LLMs (2026)

## Problem
The dominant assumption in clinical AI is that larger, more accurate models are automatically safer — that scaling accuracy is the same as scaling safety. In high-stakes medical contexts, this assumption is dangerous: a single high-confidence wrong answer that contradicts clinical evidence can cause patient harm regardless of average benchmark accuracy. No clinical LLM benchmark had separately tracked safety-critical failure modes (high-risk errors, evidence-contradicting outputs, dangerous overconfidence) independently of aggregate accuracy, leaving the accuracy-safety scaling relationship unmeasured.

## Method
**RadSaFE-200** (arXiv: 2605.04039, May 2026) introduces a Radiology Safety-Focused Evaluation benchmark comprising **200 multiple-choice questions** with clinician-defined labels for three safety-critical failure modes: high-risk errors, unsafe answers, and evidence-contradicting outputs. **34 locally deployed language models** are evaluated across **six deployment configurations**, including standard inference, agentic RAG, and clean-evidence-augmented inference. Safety metrics are tracked independently of accuracy to measure whether the two improve together or diverge with scale and configuration.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 200 radiology multiple-choice questions with clinician-defined safety labels
- Three safety-critical failure categories: high-risk errors / unsafe answers / evidence contradictions
- 34 locally deployed language models
- Six deployment configurations: standard / agentic RAG / clean evidence / others
- Domain: clinical radiology, high-stakes medical decision-making

## Key Results

| Condition | Mean Accuracy | High-Risk Errors | Contradictions | Dangerous Overconfidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard inference | 73.5% | 12.0% | 12.7% | 8.0% |
| Clean evidence provided | **94.1%** | **2.6%** | **2.3%** | **1.6%** |
| Standard/Agentic RAG | Higher than baseline | No improvement | No improvement | No improvement |

- **Safety and accuracy follow different scaling laws — standard and agentic RAG improve accuracy but fail to replicate safety improvements achieved by providing clean clinical evidence**
- Clean evidence provision reduces high-risk errors from 12.0% to 2.6%, contradictions from 12.7% to 2.3%, and dangerous overconfidence from 8.0% to 1.6% — while simultaneously improving accuracy from 73.5% to 94.1%
- Agentic RAG achieves accuracy gains but does not reduce safety-critical failure rates — the retrieval and reasoning pipeline does not guarantee evidence quality
- The accuracy-safety decoupling means organisations cannot use benchmark accuracy as a proxy for clinical deployment safety

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn's FoxBrain deployments in occupational health, manufacturing safety assessment, and worker medical support contexts face an identical safety-accuracy decoupling risk. A FoxBrain model that achieves high benchmark accuracy on medical or safety queries may still produce high-risk, evidence-contradicting outputs at clinically dangerous rates. RadSaFE-200's finding that clean evidence provision — not model scale or agentic RAG — is the primary driver of safety improvement has a direct implication for FoxBrain: the highest-ROI safety intervention is ensuring that FoxBrain has access to verified, high-quality evidence sources (not just retrieval from any available document) before generating safety-critical outputs. Standard RAG pipelines that retrieve from unverified internal documents will not reduce dangerous overconfidence in FoxBrain's safety-sensitive outputs.
