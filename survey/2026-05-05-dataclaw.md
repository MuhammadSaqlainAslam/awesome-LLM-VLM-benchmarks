# DataClaw: A Process-Oriented Agent Benchmark for Exploratory Real-World Data Analysis (2026)

## Problem
Data analysis agent benchmarks evaluate final-answer correctness on clean, synthetic datasets — but real enterprise data analysis is exploratory and iterative, performed on messy real-world data with unknown structure and realistic imperfections. Current benchmarks measure whether agents arrive at the right answer, not whether their reasoning process is sound, which sub-tasks they complete correctly, or where their analytical process breaks down. This process-blindness prevents targeted improvement and produces misleadingly high benchmark scores on tasks that would fail in production.

## Method
**DataClaw** (arXiv: 2605.02503, May 2026) introduces a process-oriented benchmark comprising approximately **2.06 million authentic records** spanning enterprise, industrial, and policy sectors, paired with **492 cross-domain analytical tasks** annotated with **intermediate checkpoints** for step-by-step assessment. Eight advanced LLMs are evaluated not just on final correctness but on reasoning progression through each checkpoint — revealing where in the analytical process agents fail, not just whether they ultimately succeed.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- ~2.06 million authentic records (enterprise / industrial / policy sectors)
- 492 cross-domain analytical tasks
- Intermediate checkpoint annotations for process-level evaluation
- 8 advanced LLMs evaluated
- Real-world messy data (realistic imperfections, unknown structure)

## Key Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Models achieving below 50% overall accuracy | **7 of 8** |
| Process-level vs. final-answer assessment | Substantially different failure patterns |
| Domain coverage | Enterprise / Industrial / Policy |
| Data realism | 2.06M authentic records with real imperfections |

- **7 of 8 advanced LLMs score below 50% overall accuracy on real-world exploratory data analysis — agents that perform well on clean synthetic benchmarks fail on authentic messy enterprise data**
- Process-oriented evaluation reveals different failure patterns than final-answer scoring: agents often make correct initial steps but fail at specific intermediate analytical stages invisible to output-only evaluation
- Authentic messy data (2.06M records with realistic imperfections) is substantially harder than synthetic benchmarks — the clean-to-messy generalisation gap is severe
- Cross-domain task design (enterprise + industrial + policy) prevents domain-specific overfitting and validates generalisation claims

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn's most valuable FoxBrain use cases involve exploratory data analysis over authentic enterprise data: supply chain performance analysis, quality defect trend investigation, production yield diagnostics, and financial variance analysis — all on real, messy operational databases. DataClaw's finding that 7 of 8 LLMs score below 50% on authentic enterprise data analysis is a direct warning: FoxBrain's performance on clean benchmark data analysis does not predict its reliability on Foxconn's actual operational data. The process-oriented evaluation methodology is directly adoptable as FoxBrain's data analysis audit approach — rather than evaluating only whether FoxBrain produces the correct final figure, Foxconn should instrument intermediate checkpoints (data loading, cleaning, aggregation, interpretation) to identify exactly where FoxBrain's analytical reasoning breaks down in real Foxconn datasets.
