# BTF-2: Bench to the Future 2 — Evaluating Strategic Reasoning in Forecasting Agents (2026)

## Problem
Forecasting benchmarks measure prediction accuracy but cannot explain why some agents outperform others — they produce leaderboards, not diagnostics. Without understanding which specific reasoning capabilities drive accuracy differences, it is impossible to systematically improve forecasting agents or understand their failure modes. Existing benchmarks also suffer from reproducibility problems and cannot isolate agent behaviour from data access differences.

## Method
**BTF-2** (arXiv: 2604.26106, April 30, 2026) introduces Bench to the Future 2, a forecasting agent benchmark comprising 1,417 past-casting questions with a frozen 15-million-document research corpus that gives all agents identical information access. This enables reproducible comparison of agent reasoning strategies rather than information advantages. The benchmark is sensitive enough to detect Brier score differences as small as 0.004. Multiple frontier AI agents are evaluated and a combined forecaster is constructed to identify capability complementarities.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 1,417 past-casting questions
- Frozen 15-million-document research corpus (identical for all agents)
- Sensitivity: detects Brier score differences ≥ 0.004
- Multiple frontier AI agents evaluated
- Domain: strategic reasoning in probabilistic forecasting

## Key Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Combined forecaster Brier improvement | +0.011 over best single agent |
| Minimum detectable accuracy difference | 0.004 Brier score |
| Critical frontier agent failure | Assessing political/business incentives |
| Critical frontier agent failure | Institutional behaviour modelling |

- **Frontier AI agents critically fail at assessing political and business leader incentives and modelling institutional behaviour — the most strategically important forecasting tasks**
- A combined forecaster (+0.011 Brier over any single agent) demonstrates that current frontier agents have complementary weaknesses, no single model dominates
- Frozen corpus design enables genuine agent comparison: performance differences reflect reasoning strategy, not information access
- BTF-2's 0.004 Brier sensitivity makes it able to detect meaningful differences that prior benchmarks mask

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn's strategic planning functions — supply chain risk forecasting, market demand prediction, geopolitical sourcing decisions — rely on reasoning about exactly the capabilities where frontier agents fail: predicting how political actors, institutional bodies, and business counterparts will behave. BTF-2's finding that LLM forecasting agents critically underperform on incentive modelling and institutional reasoning is a direct warning for any FoxBrain deployment in strategic scenario planning. Using LLM agents for supply chain geopolitical risk analysis or policy impact forecasting without human expert validation of the institutional reasoning component will produce systematically biased predictions — the agent's factual research capability is strong, but its judgment about how actors respond to incentives is unreliable.
