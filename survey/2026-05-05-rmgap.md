# RMGAP: Benchmarking the Generalization of Reward Models Across Diverse Preferences (2026)

## Problem
Reward models (RMs) are the backbone of RLHF alignment — they encode human preferences and are used to score model outputs during training and inference-time reranking (Best-of-N). Existing reward model benchmarks assume a single universal preference ordering, but in practice different users, domains, and use cases have legitimately different preferences for the same response. A reward model that cannot generalise across diverse preferences will misalign when deployed outside its training preference distribution — a critical failure mode for enterprise deployments serving heterogeneous users.

## Method
**RMGAP** (arXiv: 2605.01831, May 2026) introduces a benchmark of **1,097 instances** spanning four domains: Chat, Writing, Reasoning, and Safety. For each prompt, four distinct responses with different linguistic characteristics are generated. Each instance includes tailored preference prompts emphasising specific preference dimensions, with **two paraphrased variants** per prompt to capture natural expression variation. **24 state-of-the-art reward models** are evaluated on their ability to correctly rank responses according to the specified diverse preferences.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 1,097 instances across Chat / Writing / Reasoning / Safety domains
- 4 responses per prompt (distinct linguistic characteristics)
- Tailored preference prompts + 2 paraphrased variants per instance
- 24 state-of-the-art reward models evaluated
- Primary metric: Best-of-N accuracy

## Key Results

| Metric | Result |
|---|---|
| Best reward model Best-of-N accuracy | **49.27%** |
| Best-of-N accuracy gap to ideal | ~50 percentage points |
| Domain coverage | Chat / Writing / Reasoning / Safety |
| Models evaluated | 24 state-of-the-art reward models |

- **The best of 24 state-of-the-art reward models achieves only 49.27% Best-of-N accuracy — barely better than random on diverse preference generalisation**
- All 24 tested reward models show substantial performance gaps when preferences deviate from their training distribution — reward model generalisation is a widespread, unsolved problem
- Current RLHF-aligned models are optimised for a narrow preference distribution and fail to serve heterogeneous user populations reliably
- RMGAP is the first benchmark to separate reward model performance across legitimately different but valid preference types (rather than binary good/bad judgments)

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn's FoxBrain deployment serves a highly heterogeneous user base: manufacturing engineers, procurement specialists, HR teams, finance analysts, and executive management all have legitimately different preferences for how information should be presented, how detailed responses should be, and what constitutes a high-quality answer. RMGAP's finding that the best reward model achieves only 49.27% Best-of-N accuracy on diverse preferences means that FoxBrain's RLHF alignment — tuned to a generic preference distribution — will systematically misalign for many Foxconn user groups. This has direct implications for FoxBrain's preference fine-tuning strategy: a single reward model trained on generic human feedback cannot reliably serve Foxconn's diverse internal user population; domain-specific reward models (one for engineering, one for finance, one for HR) are likely required for reliable preference alignment across FoxBrain's user base.
