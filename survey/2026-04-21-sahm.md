# SAHM: A Benchmark for Arabic Financial and Shari'ah-Compliant Reasoning (2026)

## Problem
Arabic financial NLP is severely underrepresented in existing evaluation benchmarks, leaving LLMs deployed in Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) and broader Middle Eastern financial markets without rigorous assessment tools. Standard financial benchmarks do not cover AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions) standards, fatwa-based compliance reasoning, or Shari'ah-compliant investment evaluation — tasks uniquely required in Islamic finance contexts.

## Method
**SAHM** (arXiv: 2604.19098, April 21, 2026) provides 14,380 expert-verified instances across seven tasks: AAOIFI standards Q&A, fatwa-based Q&A and multiple-choice questions, accounting and business exams, financial sentiment analysis, extractive summarization, and event-cause reasoning. The benchmark evaluates 19 open and proprietary LLMs and finds that language fluency alone does not guarantee financial reasoning capability, with the largest performance gaps on causal reasoning tasks.

Authors: Rania Elbadry, Sarfraz Ahmad, Ahmed Heakl, Dani Bouch, Momina Ahsan, Muhra AlMahri, Marwa Elsaid Khalil, Yuxia Wang, Salem Lahlou, Sophia Ananiadou, Veselin Stoyanov, Jimin Huang, Xueqing Peng, Preslav Nakov, Zhuohan Xie

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 14,380 expert-verified instances across 7 task types
- Tasks: AAOIFI standards QA, fatwa QA, accounting exams, sentiment analysis, summarization, event-cause reasoning
- 19 LLMs evaluated (open and proprietary)
- Coverage of Islamic finance regulatory frameworks not found in existing benchmarks

## Key Results

| Task Category | Model Performance Pattern |
|---|---|
| Recognition tasks (sentiment, MCQ) | Strongest — models perform relatively well |
| Generation tasks (summarization, QA) | Moderate — significant quality gaps |
| Event-cause reasoning | Weakest — largest performance gaps |

- **Models perform substantially better on recognition-style tasks than on generation or causal reasoning, with the largest gaps on event-cause reasoning**
- Language fluency in Arabic does not transfer to financial domain reasoning capability
- All 19 LLMs show meaningful degradation relative to recognition baselines when tested on causal and generative tasks

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
Foxconn has significant manufacturing and supply chain operations in the Middle East, particularly in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, where Islamic finance instruments govern supplier credit, equipment leasing, and project financing. FoxBrain could support Foxconn's treasury and legal teams in evaluating Shari'ah compliance of financial instruments in regional procurement contracts. SAHM's event-cause reasoning tasks are particularly relevant for FoxBrain's supply chain risk analysis use case, where understanding causal relationships between financial events (e.g., commodity price shocks, regulatory changes, payment defaults) is critical for proactive risk management across the GCC supplier network.
