# PSI-Bench: Towards Clinically Grounded and Interpretable Evaluation of Depression Patient Simulators (2026)

## Problem
LLM-based patient simulators are increasingly used to train clinical AI systems and mental health chatbots, but no rigorous benchmark existed for evaluating their clinical fidelity. Existing evaluations focus on conversation quality rather than clinical realism — missing critical behavioural properties such as response length authenticity, emotional trajectory realism, lexical diversity, and behavioural diversity across simulated patient profiles. Without clinical grounding, patient simulators may train AI systems on behaviourally unrealistic interactions.

## Method
**PSI-Bench** (arXiv: 2604.25840, April 29, 2026) introduces a clinically grounded evaluation framework for depression patient simulators, assessing performance across three diagnostic levels: turn-level (individual response properties), dialogue-level (conversation arc fidelity), and population-level (diversity across patient profiles). Seven large language models are benchmarked across two simulator frameworks. A human study with clinical experts validates that PSI-Bench scores align strongly with expert judgment.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- Three diagnostic evaluation levels: turn-level / dialogue-level / population-level
- Seven LLMs evaluated across two simulator frameworks
- Metrics: response length / lexical diversity / emotional resolution speed / emotional trajectory patterns / behavioural diversity
- Expert clinical validation via human study
- Domain: depression patient simulation for clinical AI training

## Key Results

| Evaluation Dimension | Key Finding |
|---|---|
| Response length | Simulators produce excessively long responses vs. real patients |
| Lexical diversity | Abnormally high — unrealistic compared to real patient speech |
| Emotional resolution speed | Emotions resolve too quickly in simulation |
| Emotional trajectory | Uniform negative-to-positive arcs — unrealistic |
| Behavioural diversity | Reduced variability across simulated patient profiles |
| Framework vs. model impact | Simulator framework has greater fidelity impact than model scale |

- **All seven tested LLMs generate clinically unrealistic patient simulations — responses are too long, too lexically diverse, and emotions resolve too quickly and too uniformly**
- The simulator framework architecture has a greater impact on clinical fidelity than model scale — bigger models in a poor framework produce worse simulations than smaller models in a well-designed framework
- PSI-Bench scores align strongly with expert clinical assessments, validating the benchmark as a reliable proxy for clinical realism
- These findings warn that AI systems trained on current patient simulators are learning from behaviourally flawed data

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
While PSI-Bench targets clinical mental health simulation, its core methodology — multi-level behavioural fidelity evaluation of LLM-generated personas — is directly applicable to FoxBrain's use of simulated user personas in enterprise training, onboarding simulations, and customer interaction testing. The finding that the simulator framework matters more than model scale guides FoxBrain's simulation pipeline design: investing in realistic behaviour specification and framework architecture yields more fidelity than simply upgrading to a larger model. Any FoxBrain deployment that uses LLM-simulated human interactions for training or testing must validate behavioural fidelity at turn, dialogue, and population levels before relying on the simulation data.
