# TokenHD: Scalable Token-Level Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models (2026)

## Problem
LLM hallucinations frequently appear as coherent text that is factually wrong in specific tokens or spans, not as obviously incoherent output. Existing hallucination detection methods operate at the step or sentence level — they require predefined step segmentation, which limits granularity and scalability. Detecting hallucinations at step boundaries cannot pinpoint which specific tokens are hallucinated within a step, preventing targeted remediation and fine-grained quality control. Scaling hallucination detection to large, free-form model outputs without step segmentation has remained an open problem.

## Method
**TokenHD** (arXiv: 2605.12384, May 2026) introduces a two-component hallucination detection pipeline: (1) a **scalable data engine** that synthesises hallucination annotations at the token level, eliminating the need for manual step segmentation; (2) a **training recipe with importance-weighted strategies** for training robust detectors on the synthesised annotations. The pipeline operates on free-form text without requiring any step-boundary annotations or predefined structure. Detectors are trained and evaluated across sizes from **0.6B to 8B parameters**, demonstrating consistent performance scaling.

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- Synthetically annotated token-level hallucination dataset (generated by data engine)
- Free-form text evaluation (no step segmentation required)
- Detector sizes evaluated: 0.6B to 8B parameters
- Comparison baseline: QwQ-32B reasoning model

## Key Results

| Model | Hallucination Detection Performance |
|---|---|
| QwQ-32B (reasoning model baseline) | Surpassed by 0.6B TokenHD detector |
| TokenHD 0.6B | Exceeds QwQ-32B |
| TokenHD 8B | Strongest performance; scales consistently from 0.6B |

- **A 0.6B TokenHD detector trained on synthesised token-level annotations surpasses QwQ-32B — a model 53× larger — on hallucination detection**
- Detection performance scales consistently across model sizes from 0.6B to 8B — TokenHD training converts any small model into a high-quality hallucination detector
- Token-level granularity enables pinpointing specific hallucinated spans rather than flagging entire steps, enabling surgical quality control and targeted re-generation
- No step segmentation required — applicable to any free-form LLM output pipeline without output structure constraints

## Enterprise / Industry Relevance
FoxBrain produces free-form text outputs across quality reports, supplier evaluations, regulatory compliance summaries, and engineering analysis — all high-stakes contexts where hallucinated token spans (e.g., an incorrect specification value, a wrong standard number, a fabricated supplier credential) can cause operational harm. TokenHD's finding that a 0.6B detector beats QwQ-32B at token-level hallucination detection is directly actionable: Foxconn can deploy a lightweight 0.6B TokenHD detector alongside FoxBrain as a post-generation quality gate, flagging specific hallucinated spans for human review without adding significant compute overhead. Token-level granularity is particularly valuable for Foxconn's engineering and compliance contexts where the specific wrong token (e.g., "IEC 62368-1" vs. "IEC 62368-3") is the locus of risk, not the surrounding text.
