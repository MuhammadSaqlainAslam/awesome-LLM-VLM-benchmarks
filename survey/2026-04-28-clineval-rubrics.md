# ClinEval Rubrics: Case-Specific Rubrics for Clinical AI Evaluation — Methodology, Validation, and LLM-Clinician Agreement Across 823 Encounters (2026)

## Problem
Evaluating clinical AI systems at scale requires consistent, case-specific quality assessment — but clinician-authored rubrics are expensive and slow to produce, creating a bottleneck for continuous AI quality monitoring in healthcare settings. Existing evaluation methods lack the case-specificity to differentiate high- and low-quality AI outputs across diverse clinical specialties, and no study had validated whether LLM-generated rubrics can match the reliability of clinician-authored ones at a fraction of the cost.

## Method
**ClinEval Rubrics** (arXiv: 2604.24710, April 28, 2026) presents a methodology for clinical AI evaluation using case-specific rubrics authored either by clinicians or generated by LLMs. The study validates this approach across 823 clinical cases (736 real-world, 87 synthetic) spanning primary care, psychiatry, oncology, and behavioral health. Twenty clinicians authored 1,646 rubrics total. Seven versions of an EHR-embedded AI agent are evaluated. LLM-clinician ranking agreement (tau: 0.42–0.46) is compared against clinician-clinician inter-rater agreement (tau: 0.38–0.43).

## Benchmarks / Datasets
- 823 clinical cases: 736 real-world + 87 synthetic
- Four clinical domains: primary care / psychiatry / oncology / behavioral health
- 1,646 case-specific rubrics authored by 20 clinicians
- 7 versions of an EHR-embedded clinical AI agent evaluated
- LLM-generated rubrics tested as low-cost alternative to clinician-authored rubrics

## Key Results

| Evaluation Dimension | Key Finding |
|---|---|
| Rubric discriminability | Median score gap 82.9% between high- and low-quality outputs |
| AI agent improvement | Median scores improved from 84% → 95% across agent versions |
| LLM-clinician agreement | tau: 0.42–0.46 |
| Clinician-clinician agreement | tau: 0.38–0.43 |
| Cost reduction | LLM rubric generation ~1,000× cheaper than clinician-authored |

- **LLM-generated case-specific rubrics achieve ranking agreement with clinicians (tau: 0.42–0.46) that matches or exceeds clinician-clinician agreement (tau: 0.38–0.43) — at approximately 1,000× lower cost**
- Case-specific rubrics effectively differentiate high- and low-quality AI outputs with an 82.9% median score gap, confirming strong discriminability
- The methodology tracked AI agent quality improvement from 84% to 95% across 7 agent versions, demonstrating its sensitivity to real performance changes
- Ceiling compression at high performance levels presents a methodological consideration for inter-rater studies at the frontier of AI quality

## FoxBrain Relevance

ClinEval Rubrics' demonstration that LLM-generated case-specific rubrics match clinician-level evaluation reliability at 1,000× lower cost provides a direct model for FoxBrain's own quality evaluation pipeline.
